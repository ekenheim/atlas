- **The next effort's likely shape** (Codex's second review, "what are we lacking", shared by the owner 2026-10-02; the lead agrees and ranks it here, for the verdict ticket to confirm or replace). The product: a short, defensible answer to "what must be true for this company to benefit, which parts have we established, and what should we investigate next?" In order:
  1. **The bottleneck Hypothesis as a structured argument:** what is constrained (units, period); demand against qualified supply; time to relief (capacity, second source, substitute); who controls the scarce capability; how it reaches that company's revenue or margin; what would invalidate it. Each step with its supporting Evidence, its counterevidence and a status (supported, disputed, unknown), so a story resting on one unsupported step shows it. Extends the Hypothesis's mechanism fields; replaces free-text findings as the card's centre.
  2. **The next research action chosen by the most consequential unknown step** (find pricing terms when the shortage is already established), by simple rules first.
  3. **Meaning checked where a finding becomes a conclusion:** the actor, direction, tense (reported, expected, asked) and certainty of each cited Claim preserved in the finding; an inference names its premises. Pilot fix 21 checks names and figures only. **Started now at no cost:** each verdict run's review keeps its wrong Claims and misstated findings, with the reason, as evaluation cases for this check (the review pack already produces them).
  4. **Economic capture:** pricing arrangements, customer bargaining power, capacity cost, qualification time, financing and dilution, duration; the scenario separates incremental exposure from the existing business and names the assumption that drives it.
  5. **Disconfirmation per premise,** on the same companies and horizon as the support (new entrants, qualification progress, substitutes, inventory, cancellations, capacity additions). The release ships only the disclosure (pilot-fix 25's split).
  6. **A prospective loop:** predictions with a measure, a deadline, a range and a settling source, operational first (capacity online, qualification, lead times, margins, financing); rejected Hypotheses recorded too. This is the "tested against what then happened" of the scope rule, and the product spec's §9.
  None of these is built before the verdict; they are why the verdict must name one workflow that saves the owner work, since that workflow is what this argument is built on.

- **The universe grows with the theories (owner, 2026-10-02).** Each committed Candidate costs a bounded catch-up (about 250 sections for two years of history, roughly 3 hours of the retain budget), then about 10 sections a month. Investigations stay capped (6 Investigators a round), so research cost does not grow with the universe. What does not yet scale: (1) the nightly CronJob names its companies, so a committed Candidate gets its first ingest but no nightly ingest after it (fix: the nightly loop reads the stored universe, `universe_company` included); (2) no company leaves the universe when its theory dies (an `inactive` state: no ingest, its memory kept); (3) a new company's first ingest takes two years when a theory may need only the latest 10-K, 10-Q and calls (a shallower first ingest, deepened on demand); (4) the owner's commit review per Candidate. Parked until the verdict, because no Candidate has been committed yet. (1) comes first once one is.
- **Sivers Semiconductors, suggested by the owner (2026-10-03).** It was one of the four alternates in the seed-list research (`.scratch/atlas-phase3-6a/issues/01-seed-list.md`), in the chip-laser layer (InP lasers; light sources for co-packaged optics). On 2026-10-03 production had no Candidate, no lead and no memory naming it. It waits until after the verdict runs, because the universe stays frozen while they run. Then it is the first owner-sourced Candidate and the first test of the path above. It is a Nasdaq Stockholm company, not an SEC filer, and Atlas has no Swedish source adapter, so it starts with manual imports (the owner downloads the latest annual and quarterly reports; `atlas sources import`). An adapter comes only if the theory survives. Owner-sourced, so it is excluded from the Serenity answer key's "found" count (ticket 02).
- **The retain budget counts operations, not MiniMax tokens (2026-10-02, a failure).** The lead raised `ATLAS_RETAIN_BUDGET_OPERATIONS` from 200 to 400 (home-ops #7205) on the belief that MiniMax was not the constraint. That belief was based on Atlas's role-call token count, which leaves out Hindsight's extraction tokens. Within 30 minutes MiniMax's Token Plan rate limit was hit (429, code 2062), and it was reverted (#7206). Atlas did not pause, because the 429s happened inside Hindsight. Rule until fixed: retain operations stay at 200 per 5 h, which ran cleanly. The fix, after the verdict: budget retains by the tokens Hindsight reports for each operation (or Hindsight's own usage, read from LiteLLM), and pause on an extraction 429 the way a role-call 429 pauses.
- **Retain format changes re-retain the whole bank,** so each costs more as the bank grows: change it rarely, batch the changes, and only on a measured gain (the 0.4.1 backfill is the first).
